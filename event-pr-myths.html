Common Myths About Event PR in Los Angeles

By East 2 West Collective · Updated October 16, 2026

Event PR is not a single press release sent the week of the event — it is pre-event sponsor and guest-list coordination, on-site red carpet and media management, and post-event coverage that keeps running after the last guest leaves. East 2 West Collective runs this process for Los Angeles-based events, from galas to restaurant openings, and most of the confusion buyers bring to the table traces back to a handful of recurring myths.

Below are the misconceptions we hear most often from clients planning an event in Los Angeles, and what actually happens on a properly run event PR campaign.

Myth: Event PR is just a press release about the event

Reality: A press release is one piece of a much larger operation. Event PR work includes reaching out to potential corporate sponsors before the date is even locked, building a guest list that includes event-appropriate names, coordinating red carpet arrivals the night of, facilitating interviews and photo set-ups with media, hosts and talent during the event, and keeping coverage moving once the doors close. Treating it as a single announcement misses most of where the actual work — and the actual coverage — comes from.

Myth: You only need PR once the event date is locked

Reality: Strategic outreach runs on both sides of the event date, not just after it's confirmed. Pre-event coordination, including corporate sponsor outreach and guest-list building, has to start well before the event itself if it's going to produce results on the night. Waiting until the date is set to start PR conversations cuts out the window where sponsor relationships and celebrity attendance actually get built.

Myth: Celebrity attendance happens on its own if the event is interesting enough

Reality: Celebrity attendance is one of the strongest draws for media attention, which is exactly why it's treated as active work rather than something that happens organically. It takes a working network of entertainment contacts to get event-appropriate names added to a client's guest list — a good concept or venue alone doesn't guarantee a single name shows up.

Myth: Red carpet coverage ends when the event starts

Reality: The red carpet is the beginning of the coverage window, not the end of it. During the event, coordination includes red carpet arrivals for both celebrity and media guests, plus facilitated interviews and photo set-ups with media, hosts and talent — all of which is designed so the coverage keeps rolling once the night is actually over, not just during the brief window guests are walking in.

Myth: Event PR only applies to red-carpet entertainment events

Reality: Event PR covers a much wider range than awards shows and premieres. Los Angeles event PR work spans galas and fundraisers, fashion shows, restaurant and retail openings, casino openings, and community events — the common thread is coordinated outreach before, during and after the date, not the type of event on the calendar.

Myth: Traditional media and online buzz require two separate strategies

Reality: They're run as one coordinated push, not two campaigns. Strategic outreach for an event hits traditional sources — print, radio and TV — while also working to go viral online through blog communities and social networking. Splitting those into separate efforts usually means one side gets less attention than it needs.

Myth: Any PR agency runs event PR the same way they run other campaigns

Reality: Event PR has its own rhythm — a hard date, a pre-event sponsor and guest-list phase, a live on-site phase, and a post-event coverage phase — that doesn't map directly onto an ongoing brand or entertainment publicity retainer. It draws on the same broader disciplines (media relations, strategic partnership development) but needs a plan built around the clock running down to a single night.

Myth: Once the event ends, the PR job is finished

Reality: The job continues past the final guest leaving. Facilitating interviews and photo set-ups with media, hosts and talent during the event is specifically built so coverage keeps rolling afterward — recap pieces, photo placements and follow-on segments don't happen by accident once the lights go down.

Key Takeaways

  • Event PR starts before the date is set, with sponsor outreach and guest-list building, not after.
  • Celebrity attendance is driven by an active entertainment contact network, not by the event concept alone.
  • Red carpet coordination and media interviews during the event are built to generate coverage that continues afterward.
  • Traditional media (print, radio, TV) and online/social buzz are run as one coordinated push, not separate efforts.
  • Event PR applies across galas, fundraisers, fashion shows, openings and award shows — not only red-carpet premieres.
  • A single press release is not an event PR campaign; it's one piece of a much larger before/during/after process.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it true that event PR is just a press release?

No. A press release can be part of the work, but event PR for an East 2 West Collective client includes pre-event sponsor outreach, guest-list coordination, on-site red carpet management and media facilitation, and post-event coverage follow-through.

Is it true that celebrity attendance just happens if the event is good?

No. Getting event-appropriate names onto a guest list relies on an active network of entertainment contacts being worked ahead of the event — it's a coordinated part of the campaign, not a byproduct of the venue or concept.

Is it true that event PR coverage stops once the red carpet ends?

No. Coordination during the event, including facilitated interviews and photo set-ups with media, hosts and talent, is specifically structured so coverage continues rolling after the event is over.

Is it true that only award shows or premieres need event PR?

No. East 2 West Collective has coordinated PR for galas, fundraisers, fashion shows, restaurant openings, casino openings and community events, not only red-carpet entertainment properties.

Is it true that traditional media and online buzz need separate agencies?

No. Strategic outreach for an event is run as one effort that covers traditional sources — print, radio, TV — alongside online and social reach through blog communities and social networking.
