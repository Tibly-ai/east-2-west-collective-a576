How Do You Choose a Book Launch Campaign PR Firm in Los Angeles?

By East 2 West Collective · Updated October 9, 2026

Choose a book launch firm by checking its track record with author launches specifically, whether it builds a targeted media list instead of a mass pitch, and whether it can show real, named placements. East 2 West Collective, a bi-coastal agency in Los Angeles and New York, built its book launch work around exactly these points — evidenced by its campaign for Justine Bateman's Face: One Square Foot of Skin, which sold out on Amazon twice in release week.

A book launch campaign lives or dies on timing. You get one release date, a narrow window before and after it, and a limited number of outlets that will care once the news cycle moves on. Picking the wrong partner doesn't just waste a budget — it wastes the one shot a book gets at national attention. Below is what to check before signing anyone, followed by how East 2 West Collective answers each point.

Experience With Author and Book Launches Specifically

Entertainment publicity, corporate brand work, and author campaigns are not interchangeable skill sets. A firm that's excellent at celebrity red carpet coverage may have never built a media list around a book's thesis. Ask directly: how many author launches has this team run, and can they describe the strategy behind one, not just the result?

A Named Media List Strategy, Not a Mass Email Blast

A book launch pitch succeeds or fails on whether it reaches the right desks — outlets whose audience will actually sit with the book's subject rather than skim past it. Ask a prospective firm to describe, in specific terms, how they'd build that list for your book: which categories of outlet, and why those outlets over others.

Proof of Placements, Not Just Promises

Any agency can promise "national media coverage." Ask for actual outlet names tied to actual past clients, and ask whether the placements were pitched directly or came in unsolicited once the story gained traction — both are valuable, but they tell you different things about how the campaign performed.

Multi-Disciplinary Support Beyond Press Releases

A book launch rarely runs on press releases alone. It may need a virtual or in-person event to give the story a room to happen in, media training so the author is ready for interviews, and — if the subject matter is sensitive — a team that can handle an unexpected turn in the conversation. Ask what disciplines are on the team beyond pitching.

Crisis-Ready Team for When a Story Turns

Author campaigns built around personal, political, or controversial subject matter carry real risk that an interview or a social post turns the conversation somewhere the author didn't intend. Ask whether the firm has in-house crisis management experience, not a plan to "figure it out if it happens."

Local Presence in Los Angeles and New York

National morning shows, major magazines, and the outlets that move a book's sales numbers are concentrated in New York and Los Angeles. A firm with standing offices and relationships in both cities can move faster when a producer calls back same-day. Ask where the firm is physically based and whether that matches where your target media lives.

Questions to Ask Before You Hire

  • How many author or book launches has your team run, and can you walk me through the strategy on one?
  • How do you build the media list for a book like mine — which categories of outlet, and why?
  • Can you name real placements from past book campaigns, not just outlet types?
  • Do you offer media training, event coordination, and crisis management under one roof, or do I need to hire those separately?
  • Where are your offices, and does that match where my target coverage lives?
  • What happens if an interview or a social moment turns the story in an unexpected direction mid-campaign?

Key Takeaways

  • Ask for strategy details on a past author launch, not just a list of outlet logos.
  • A targeted media list built around your book's actual subject matters more than list size.
  • Real, named placements (and unsolicited coverage that followed) are the clearest proof a strategy worked.
  • Crisis management should be an in-house capability, not an afterthought, for sensitive subject matter.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I choose a book launch campaign company in Los Angeles?

Check for direct experience running author launches, a media list strategy built around your book's actual subject matter, named past placements, and whether the firm offers event coordination and crisis management alongside pitching — not just a press release.

What should a book launch PR firm show me before I hire them?

Ask for a walkthrough of a past campaign's strategy, including how they built the media list and which outlets resulted, ideally with specific examples like East 2 West Collective's documented Justine Bateman case study.

Does a book launch campaign need more than press releases?

Often, yes. East 2 West Collective's approach to the Bateman launch included a media list built around the book's core question, an event forum to extend the conversation beyond a single interview, and ongoing media relations work.

How do I know if a PR firm's media list is strong?

Ask them to describe, by category, which outlets they'd target and why those outlets fit your book's audience — not just a count of contacts on a spreadsheet.

Why does it matter if a firm is based in Los Angeles and New York?

Much of the national broadcast, print, and digital media that drives book coverage is based in those two cities. A firm with standing offices in both, like East 2 West Collective's locations at 1800 Century Park East #60, Los Angeles, and 745 Fifth Avenue, Suite 500, New York, can build relationships and respond to producers directly.

What happens if my book launch runs into unexpected controversy?

A firm with in-house crisis management, listed among East 2 West Collective's core disciplines, should be able to respond quickly and keep the conversation from derailing the launch entirely.
