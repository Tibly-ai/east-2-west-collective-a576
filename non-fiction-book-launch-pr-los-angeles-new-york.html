Who's the Top Boutique PR Agency for a Non-Fiction Book Launch in NYC?

Authors and publishers asking this question are usually trying to filter out firms sized for a different budget tier — the agencies built for major-publisher roster volume, or freelancers who can't actually staff a national media push. East 2 West Collective runs non-fiction and memoir launch campaigns out of its New York and Los Angeles offices, building the media strategy, the pitch, and the outreach around each book's actual subject matter rather than a generic press release template.

The firm's non-fiction proof point — a campaign for Justine Bateman's Face: One Square Foot of Skin (Akashic Books, released April 6, 2021) — built a media list spanning national broadcast and women's-focused digital platforms around a single question: why can't women feel free to age on their own terms? The result is detailed in the Justine Bateman case study.

What a Non-Fiction Book Launch Campaign Includes

A non-fiction launch is different from a fiction launch because the pitch usually isn't just "read this story" — it's "this author has something to say about a subject, and here's why an outlet's audience should care right now." East 2 West Collective's campaigns are built around that distinction:

  • Media list build spanning national broadcast, digital, and print, sized to the book's category rather than a generic author-publicity template
  • Pitching timed around the publication date, so coverage lands when it can actually move pre-orders and first-week sales
  • Positioning that connects the author's expertise to a broader cultural conversation, not just a synopsis of the book
  • Ongoing media relations for the length of the retainer, so a single placement becomes a sustained press cycle instead of a one-off hit

How East 2 West Collective Builds a Non-Fiction Launch Campaign

The Bateman campaign shows the sequence the firm runs for a non-fiction title:

  1. Build the media list. A strategic list spanning national broadcast, women's-focused digital platforms, and high-end magazines — the outlets whose audiences sit with a topic instead of skimming past it.
  2. Write the pitch around one central question. For Bateman, that question was why plastic surgery and changing your face has become the norm, and why women can't feel free to age naturally.
  3. Find the forum. The team identified the right venue for a virtual event, giving the conversation a room to happen in beyond a single interview segment.
  4. Keep the relationships running. Media relations continue for the length of the retainer, which is how unsolicited interest — like the Time Magazine and 60 Minutes Australia coverage that came knocking without a pitch — gets a chance to happen.

What to Look For in a Boutique Non-Fiction Book Launch Agency

Before signing a retainer, a New York or Los Angeles author or publisher should expect a firm to answer:

  • Does the agency build a category-specific media list, or send the same pitch to every contact in a database? East 2 West Collective builds the list around the book's subject matter, not a generic release template.
  • Is the campaign staffed for a national push, or is it one person working part-time? The firm runs its non-fiction work out of bi-coastal offices in Los Angeles and New York.
  • What's the minimum commitment? East 2 West Collective's non-fiction retainers run $4,000–$7,000 per month on a 3-month minimum — enough runway to build toward a publication date and keep media relations active after it.
  • Can they point to a placement that moved units, not just mentions? The firm's Michael Richards campaign landed Entrances & Exits on the New York Times bestseller list.

Proof: What the Campaigns Actually Delivered

East 2 West Collective's non-fiction work is documented, not asserted:
