What Event PR Covers When You're Managing a Client's Appearance

As a talent manager, you're not hiring a party planner — you're hiring whoever controls the narrative around your client the night of, and for the news cycle after. East 2 West Collective's events work runs strategic outreach on both sides of the event date: traditional press (print, radio, TV) and the online/blog/social channels that carry coverage beyond a single night. For an event your client is attached to — hosting, appearing, or being honored — that means:

  • Pre-event coordination with potential corporate sponsors, when the event has a sponsorship component.
  • Outreach into E2W's entertainment network to help round out a guest list with event-appropriate names, since celebrity attendance is one of the strongest draws for media attention.
  • On-site coordination of red carpet arrivals for both celebrity and media guests.
  • Facilitated interviews and photo set-ups with media, hosts, and talent, so coverage keeps generating after the event ends.

This sits alongside entertainment publicity work the firm does for talent year-round — brand personas built across a client's full career, not just a single appearance — which matters if the event is one stop in a longer media push you're already running for your client.

How the Engagement Works for a Talent Manager

You're the filter between your client and every outside request — including the agency's. Expect the relationship to run through you for approvals: who your client talks to, which outlets get access, what gets confirmed on the guest list. East 2 West Collective's team includes Elizabeth Much and Sydney Small as points of contact, and the firm states it becomes "true partners" with clients rather than running a templated press release and walking away.

Because the site does not publish a fixed retainer structure, minimum budget, or standard onboarding timeline for event work, the practical answer is to get this in writing early: confirm who signs off on the press list, who approves interview requests the day of, and how far in advance the team needs your client's availability locked. Ask these as part of the first call rather than assuming a standard process — the facts on this point aren't published, so don't take a default for granted from any agency, including this one.

What a Talent Manager Should Ask Before Signing On

Before committing a client's name to an event campaign, a few questions protect both your client and your own time:

  1. Who else is involved in approvals? If your client has a separate publicist, confirm up front how media requests and quotes get routed so nothing goes out without your sign-off.
  2. What's the plan if the story turns unexpected? East 2 West Collective lists crisis management among its core disciplines — ask how that applies specifically to an event-night scenario, not just a long-term brand crisis.
  3. What does "celebrity guest coordination" actually produce? Ask for specifics on how names get added to a guest list and what commitment that represents.
  4. Is this a one-night campaign or does it tie into ongoing publicity? If your client needs sustained coverage, ask how the event fits into brand marketing and consultancy work the firm runs for ongoing positioning.
  5. What record of past events can they show you? A working history of comparable events tells you more than a pitch deck.

The firm states it dials into its network of entertainment contacts to help add event-appropriate names to a guest list, and coordinates red carpet arrivals and interviews for both celebrity and media attendees on-site.
