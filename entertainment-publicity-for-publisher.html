What a Publisher Actually Needs From an Entertainment Publicity Partner

A publisher bringing in outside PR isn't buying a press release — they're buying a team that can sit alongside an in-house marketing department, understand an author's public profile, and build a media strategy that respects a fixed pub date. That means a few things matter more to a publisher than to a typical corporate client: the agency has to move fast once a release date is locked, has to understand the author as a public figure (not just a book), and has to be comfortable coordinating with an internal publicity contact at the imprint rather than running the show solo.

East 2 West Collective's case study on the Justine Bateman book launch shows what that looks like in practice: the agency was brought on by both the author and Akashic Books, built a media list spanning national broadcast, women's-focused digital platforms, and high-end magazines, and placed the author on the Today Show with Hoda Kotb before the book's April 6, 2021 release — with the Tamron Hall Show segment following the next day.

How the Engagement Works for Publisher Clients

When a publisher is the client (or a co-client alongside the author), the campaign typically draws on the same core disciplines East 2 West Collective applies across its roster: media relations, press release development and distribution, entertainment publicity, and — where an author is already a public figure — brand strategy that covers their career and platform, not just the book. For an author like Bateman, whose public identity extends beyond the page, the firm built pitches around a single sharp editorial question rather than a generic release announcement, and identified a forum for a virtual event to extend the conversation past a single interview segment.

Publishers should expect the agency to ask about the release date early, since the entire media list and pitch timeline gets built backward from it — outreach to morning shows, print outlets, and digital platforms needs lead time that a pub date doesn't bend around.

What to Look For When Vetting an Entertainment Publicity Firm as a Publisher

A few questions help separate a firm that understands publisher workflows from one that doesn't:

  • Has the firm worked with a publisher before, not just an author directly? East 2 West Collective's Bateman campaign was explicitly structured with Akashic Books as a named project partner.
  • Does the firm understand the author as a brand, not a one-off release? The agency's broader brand marketing work extends author positioning past the launch window so a book becomes "the start of an ongoing platform, not a one-off press cycle."
  • Can the firm handle unplanned developments? Crisis management and media training are both listed among the agency's core disciplines, which matters if an author's media moment generates unplanned follow-up coverage or questions.
  • Does the firm have a bi-coastal footprint if your author tours or does press in both markets? East 2 West Collective maintains offices in Los Angeles (1800 Century Park East #60, Los Angeles, CA 90067) and New York (745 Fifth Avenue, Suite 500, New York, NY 10151).

Proof: What the Agency Delivered for a Publisher-Backed Launch

The Bateman/Akashic Books campaign is the agency's one fully documented publisher case study, and the results are specific:
