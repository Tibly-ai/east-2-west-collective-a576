What Should You Expect When Hiring Crisis PR in Los Angeles?

By East 2 West Collective · Updated October 14, 2026

The approach is a fast, clear-headed response built specifically for a client's situation rather than a generic playbook, folded together with media relations, media training and brand strategy so the response holds up once cameras and comment sections move on. If you've never worked with a PR firm during a difficult moment, the process can feel opaque from the outside. This guide walks through what a first-time client should know before making that first call — no industry jargon assumed, no guesswork required.

Is Crisis Management the Right Service for Your Situation?

Crisis management, as East 2 West Collective defines it, is for "when a client's story turns unexpected" — meaning the situation is already in motion or about to be. That could mean a negative story is about to run, a public statement has drawn backlash, or a client, brand or production needs a fast, coordinated response before the narrative sets. It's distinct from day-to-day media relations, which is about proactively pitching and placing stories, and from media training, which prepares someone to walk into any interview ready and confident. If your need is ongoing visibility rather than damage control, the team's broader entertainment publicity and brand marketing work may be the better starting point — crisis management is specifically the fast-response piece.

What to Decide Before You Call

Before reaching out, it helps to have a basic sense of a few things:

  • What has already happened — a story that's published, a statement that's circulating, an internal issue at risk of going public.
  • Who is affected — an individual (talent, author, executive), a brand, or a production or company.
  • What you need most urgently — a public statement, a media strategy, internal communications guidance, or all of the above.
  • Who else needs to be looped in — legal counsel, management, a network or studio partner, internal leadership.

You don't need polished answers to all of this before you call. The point of the first conversation is to work through it together, not to arrive with a finished plan.

What to Have Ready for the First Conversation

A first-time client doesn't need a dossier, but having the following on hand speeds things up considerably:

  • Any existing coverage, screenshots, or links tied to the situation
  • A timeline of what's happened so far, even a rough one
  • Names and roles of anyone else already involved (publicists, lawyers, managers)
  • Any statements already issued or drafted
  • Contact information for decision-makers who can approve a response quickly

Crisis situations move fast, and the team's ability to act fast depends on how quickly they can get a full picture of what's actually going on.

What the First Conversation Covers

The initial call or meeting is about understanding the situation, not pitching a package. Expect the conversation to cover the facts of what's happened, who needs to be protected or represented, what channels are already involved (press, social, internal), and what outcome you're hoping for. From there, East 2 West Collective builds a response strategy specific to that situation — the site is explicit that campaigns are built as "a blueprint... tailored to the client in front of us," not a template pulled off a shelf.

What Your Estimate Will Include

Because crisis management scope varies enormously — a single statement versus a sustained multi-week response involving media relations, internal communications and brand repositioning — pricing isn't a fixed number quoted up front. What shapes the estimate is the scope of the response needed (statement-only versus full campaign), how many disciplines are involved (crisis management often pairs with media training or brand strategy & consultancy), and how quickly the situation needs to move. The clearest way to get an accurate picture is to start a conversation directly with the team so they can scope the actual situation rather than a hypothetical one.

How Long Crisis Response Typically Takes

There's no fixed timeline for crisis management, and any firm that quotes one before understanding your situation is guessing. What it depends on in practice: how contained or widespread the story already is, how many outlets or platforms are involved, whether a public statement is needed immediately or a longer communications plan is more appropriate, and whether the crisis intersects with an existing campaign (say, an entertainment client already in an active publicity cycle, or a brand mid-launch). Some of this moves in hours; some of it plays out over weeks. The team's media training discipline — preparing clients to "walk into any interview ready and confident" — is often part of a longer-tail response rather than the first 24 hours.

What to Expect on the Day

When a response is active, expect direct, frequent contact rather than a single hand-off. The team's broader working style, as described on their own site, is to become "true partners with every client," which in a crisis context typically means close coordination on messaging, approvals before anything goes out publicly, and real-time adjustments as the situation develops — not a static press release sent once and left alone.

After the Crisis: What Comes Next

Crisis response doesn't end when the immediate story cools off. The same team that handles the acute phase can also fold the situation into longer-term brand strategy & consultancy, so the messaging that worked during the crisis keeps the client's position steady afterward. For individuals and brands, this is often the point where crisis management transitions into the kind of ongoing brand strategy work described on the brand marketing page.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to already have a public crisis before I call?

No. A call is often most useful before a story fully breaks or escalates, while there's still room to shape the response. If a situation is developing, reaching out early gives the team more room to work with.

How fast can East 2 West Collective respond?

The team describes its crisis management approach as a "fast, clear-headed response," but exact timing depends on the scope and urgency of the specific situation — the first conversation is where that gets scoped.

What's the difference between crisis management and media training?

Crisis management is the strategic response to an unexpected situation as it unfolds. Media training is separate preparation that gets a client ready and confident for any interview, and the two are often used together during and after a crisis.

Do you work with individuals or only brands and companies?

East 2 West Collective works across entertainers, television properties, authors, lifestyle experts and corporate brands, so crisis management is built for individuals and organizations alike.

Can you help if the story has already broken?

Yes — crisis management is specifically designed for situations already in motion, not just ones you can see coming.

Does crisis management end once the story dies down?

Not necessarily. The team often carries a client from crisis response into ongoing brand strategy & consultancy work, so the positioning holds after the immediate pressure is gone.
