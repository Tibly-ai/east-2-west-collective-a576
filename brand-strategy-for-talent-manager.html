Brand Strategy for a Talent Manager in Los Angeles, CA

By East 2 West Collective · Updated October 10, 2026

East 2 West Collective builds brand strategy for talent managers by mapping a client's full career — acting or TV work, philanthropy, business endeavors, endorsement deals, and partnerships — into one brand persona, then refining that strategic blueprint on an ongoing basis as media exposure comes in. The firm has offices in Los Angeles and New York and has orchestrated Oscar, Emmy, Tony, and Grammy award-winning campaigns.

If you manage talent, you're not buying a press release. You're buying a strategy that has to hold up across a client's entire public footprint — the role they're promoting this quarter, the foundation they chair, the brand deal their agent just closed, and the headline risk that could undo all of it overnight. That's a different brief than a single-campaign publicist pitch, and it changes what you should expect from the agency you hire.

What a Talent Manager Actually Needs from a Brand Strategy Partner

A manager's job is to protect and grow a client's value across every revenue stream at once — acting fees, endorsements, speaking, production deals, philanthropic visibility. A brand strategy engagement that only thinks about the next press cycle doesn't serve that. East 2 West Collective's entertainment publicity approach is built around this exact problem: a brand persona that "covers the client's career, philanthropic work, business endeavors, endorsement deals and partnerships," developed as a strategic blueprint and continuously refined — not a one-size-fits-all campaign copied from the last client roster.

For a manager, that means the strategy conversation starts earlier than "we have a project to promote." It starts with the whole picture of where the client's name shows up and what each appearance is doing for (or against) the brand you're building long-term.

How the Engagement Works

East 2 West Collective's brand marketing and consultancy work folds together brand strategy, media relations, public relations, strategic partnership development, internal and crisis communications, influencer engagement, and experiential marketing — described explicitly as pieces that don't sit in a silo, each one built to reinforce the next.

For a talent manager, that integration matters operationally: it means one team is coordinating the press hit, the brand partnership pitch, and the crisis response plan, instead of three vendors who don't talk to each other. The firm's roster work spans wellness and fitness clients, authors, and fashion, in addition to its entertainment publicity practice — so the team has built brand campaigns outside a single vertical, which is relevant if your client's business interests extend past acting or TV work into a product line, a book, or a fitness brand.

The firm's team also includes strategists who specialize in television networks, individual shows, and feature films, building integrated cross-media campaigns aimed at growing viewership and market position — useful context if part of what you manage is a client attached to a specific property rather than just a personal brand.

What to Ask For When You Bring This to the Table

As the manager, you're typically the one setting the terms of an engagement before your client or their agent weighs in. A few things worth asking East 2 West Collective directly, since the published materials don't spell out retainer structure or campaign minimums:

  • The blueprint, in writing. Ask what the brand persona and strategic blueprint look like before work starts, since the model is built around a tailored plan rather than a template.
  • Who's assigned. Given the firm's specialists in network and feature film strategy, ask whether your client's situation (an actor between roles vs. talent attached to a specific show) gets a different team configuration.
  • How crisis response is handled inside the retainer. Crisis management and internal communications are both part of the integrated approach — confirm how that's triggered and staffed if something breaks mid-campaign.
  • Approval checkpoints. Since you're often the gatekeeper between the agency and your client (and sometimes the client's other representation), ask how drafts, pitches, and media lists get routed for sign-off before they go out.

None of this is published pricing or policy on the site — it's the conversation a manager should have in the first call, and East 2 West Collective is set up to have it directly with you at 310-963-8050 or emuch@e2wcollective.com.
