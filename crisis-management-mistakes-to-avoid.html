Crisis Management Mistakes to Avoid

By East 2 West Collective · Updated October 10, 2026

8 Crisis Management Mistakes to Avoid in Los Angeles

The single biggest mistake is not having a plan before the story breaks — crisis management only works as fast, clear-headed response, and that requires a relationship and a process already in place, not a scramble after the headline hits. East 2 West Collective builds that readiness into its crisis management work for entertainers, authors, lifestyle experts, and brands across Los Angeles and New York.

Crisis communications is one of eleven disciplines East 2 West Collective offers as part of its full-spectrum PR and marketing work, and it's rarely needed in isolation — a crisis touches brand strategy, media relations, and sometimes an entire campaign timeline at once. Below are the mistakes that most often turn a manageable story into a lasting one, and what to do instead.

Mistake 1: Waiting Until the Story Breaks to Build a Plan

Many clients only think about crisis management after a reporter has already called. By then, the team is reacting instead of controlling the narrative. The alternative is treating crisis readiness as part of an ongoing relationship with a PR partner — one who already understands the client's brand persona, history, and key relationships — so that when something unexpected happens, the response starts from a position of knowledge, not a cold start.

Mistake 2: Choosing a Crisis Partner on Price Alone

A crisis moment is not the moment to shop for the cheapest option. Speed and judgment matter more than almost anything else in the first hours of a story turning unexpected, and a firm that has to ramp up on the client's history while also managing the news cycle is working at a disadvantage. The better approach is selecting a firm with bi-coastal reach and direct relationships across broadcast, digital, and print — because crisis stories don't wait for one market's news cycle to clear before spreading to the other coast.

Mistake 3: Skipping the Written Scope and Strategy

Jumping straight into statements and phone calls without agreeing on the strategy first is one of the fastest ways to lose control of a story. Every East 2 West Collective engagement is built around a strategic blueprint tailored to the client in front of them, not a copied template — the same discipline that applies to entertainment publicity and brand marketing campaigns applies here. Before any statement goes out, the plan should define who speaks, what gets said, and to which outlets first.

Mistake 4: Letting One Department Write the Public Statement Alone

A statement written only by legal counsel, or only by an internal team unfamiliar with how media outlets actually use quotes, often reads as defensive or evasive — which can draw more scrutiny than the original story. Crisis communications works best as one piece of an integrated approach that also includes media relations, public relations, and internal communications, so the public statement and the internal one are never telling two different stories.

Mistake 5: Going Silent Instead of Communicating Fast

Silence is often read as guilt, even when there's a reasonable explanation. A fast, clear-headed response is the entire premise of effective crisis management — not a rushed one, but a prompt one that acknowledges the situation while the strategy is finalized. Clients who disappear during the first news cycle typically spend far longer rebuilding trust afterward than clients who address it early, even imperfectly.

Mistake 6: Treating Every Media Outlet the Same Way

Broadcast, digital, and print each carry a story differently, and a statement built for one doesn't always translate to the others. Broadcast, digital and print media relations is one of the core disciplines East 2 West Collective builds into every campaign, and during a crisis that means pitching and placing the right message with the right desk — not blasting one press release everywhere and hoping it lands correctly.

Mistake 7: Skipping Media Training Before the Interview

Sending a client into a live interview, a hostile question, or a follow-up call without preparation is one of the most common and most avoidable mistakes. Media training exists specifically to prepare clients to walk into any interview ready and confident — including the unplanned ones that come up mid-crisis. Clients who skip this step are far more likely to say something that extends the story rather than closes it.

Mistake 8: Underestimating How Fast a Story Moves Through Los Angeles' Entertainment Industry

Los Angeles is one of East 2 West Collective's two core markets, and its concentration of entertainment industry media, talent representatives, and entertainment publicity relationships means a story involving entertainers, television properties, or production companies can move through the trade and consumer press simultaneously. A plan built for a slower news cycle in another market often doesn't hold up here. The right approach accounts for Los Angeles' pace from the outset, not after the story has already spread.

Key Takeaways

  • Build a crisis communications relationship before a crisis happens, not during one.
  • Don't choose a crisis partner on price alone — speed and existing knowledge of the client matter more in the first hours.
  • Insist on a written strategic blueprint before any public statement goes out.
  • Coordinate legal, internal, and public messaging so they tell one consistent story.
  • Respond fast and clearly rather than going silent while a statement is finalized.
  • Match the message to the outlet — broadcast, digital, and print each need their own approach.
  • Complete media training before any live interview, planned or unplanned.
  • Account for how quickly a story can move through the Los Angeles entertainment press specifically.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it a mistake to handle a crisis statement internally without a PR partner?

It can be, especially for entertainers, authors, or brands with existing media exposure. Crisis management is one of East 2 West Collective's core disciplines because statements written without media relations experience often read as defensive and can extend a story rather than close it.

What if the story breaks before we've built a relationship with a PR firm?

A fast, clear-headed response is still possible, but it starts from a weaker position than a client who already has a strategic blueprint in place. The sooner a firm is brought in, the sooner the response can be coordinated across legal, internal, and public messaging.

Is it a mistake to say nothing until legal clears every word?

Waiting too long to communicate is itself a risk — silence is frequently read as guilt. The better path is coordinating legal review with a PR strategist from the start, so the response is both accurate and fast rather than accurate and late.

What if the crisis involves both our Los Angeles and New York operations?

This is exactly the kind of situation a bi-coastal firm is built for. East 2 West Collective operates in both Los Angeles and New York, which matters when a story crosses markets rather than staying contained to one news cycle.

Is it a mistake to skip media training if the client has done interviews before?

Yes — a crisis interview is different from a routine press interview, and media training specifically prepares clients to walk into any interview ready and confident, including the harder, unplanned questions that come up during a crisis.
