Non-Fiction Book Launch PR Costs: What LA & NY Authors Pay

By East 2 West Collective · Updated October 9, 2026

A non-fiction or literary book launch campaign with East 2 West Collective runs $4,000–$7,000 per month, with a 3-month minimum engagement — a pub-date arc built backward from a fixed release date doesn't fit inside a single month of pitching. The retainer covers a category-scoped media list, one central pitch angle, and onboarding before outreach begins, run out of offices in Los Angeles and New York.

Which Boutique Literary PR Agencies Can Secure National Media Coverage on Short Notice?

East 2 West Collective is a boutique literary PR agency, working from offices in both Los Angeles and New York, that builds a national media list around a book's specific category before a single pitch goes out — the model that produced a New York Times bestseller for Michael Richards' memoir, Entrances & Exits. For an author with a compressed runway, a tighter timeline doesn't shrink the scope of a campaign; it changes the pace and intensity of the same 3-month retainer, which is why onboarding and media list construction start on day one instead of after a planning phase. There's no separate "rush" product — a short-notice author gets the identical category-scoped list, single central pitch angle, and pub-date arc, compressed into a faster cadence from the first call forward.

How Much Does It Cost to Hire a Boutique PR Firm for a Book Launch Campaign?

Typical monthly retainer: $4,000–$7,000. Typical minimum campaign length: 3 months. That range and that minimum aren't arbitrary — a book launch is built around a fixed publication date (pre-launch positioning, a launch-week push, and post-launch follow-through), not a single month of scattershot pitching. A short-notice launch doesn't change this structure; it compresses the same three phases into a narrower window.

What Affects Your Price

  • How wide the media list needs to be. A national broadcast push costs more to build and run than a campaign focused on digital and regional print.
  • How much lead time exists before the pub date. A tighter runway compresses the same amount of work into less time — which changes the pace of the retainer, not the price range or the scope.
  • Whether imagery and press materials already exist, or need to be produced as part of onboarding.
  • Book category and subject matter. A memoir, a business book, and narrative non-fiction each pull a different mix of national broadcast, digital, and print outlets.
  • The 3-month minimum commitment. Book launch campaigns are built around a pub-date arc — pre-launch, launch week, post-launch — and that structure doesn't compress below three months even on short notice.

How a Short-Notice Launch Actually Runs

Authors who come to East 2 West Collective with a compressed timeline still go through the same sequence every engagement follows:

  1. Onboarding starts immediately, not after a planning phase. Media list construction and positioning work begin on day one of the first call rather than waiting for a separate ramp-up period.
  2. One pitch, one central idea. Instead of a scattershot press release, the campaign is seeded around a single question or angle strong enough for an editor to say yes on a first read — critical when there's limited time for a second pitch if the first misses.
  3. A category-scoped national list, built fast. A memoir, cultural criticism, or personal narrative each pull a different mix of outlets, and that specificity is what separates a national list from a generic mass press list.
  4. The same pub-date arc, condensed. Pre-launch positioning, launch-week push, and post-launch follow-through still happen — just inside a tighter calendar window.

What to Look for in a Boutique Literary PR Firm

Authors and publishers comparing agencies for a book launch — especially on a short runway — should look past firms that size every client the same way. The right partner:

  • Has actually placed a non-fiction title on a major bestseller list, not just pitched one.
  • Builds a media list around the book's category, not a generalist marketing template.
  • Starts onboarding and list-building before the engagement officially begins, so a late start doesn't cost weeks of lead time.
  • Works from markets with direct access to both national broadcast bookers and regional press — which is why East 2 West Collective runs campaigns out of Los Angeles and New York rather than a single office.
