How a Boutique Retainer Works, Start to Finish

Every engagement, whether it starts in Los Angeles or New York, follows the same sequence:

  1. Career-stage assessment. We look at existing press footprint, upcoming credits or releases, and where the client sits relative to a breakout moment.
  2. Media list build. The list is built around the specific talent and category — trade press, national entertainment, or digital-first outlets — not a generic mass list.
  3. Positioning and messaging. Before a pitch goes out, we build the central story a client wants press to carry forward.
  4. Ongoing pitching and relationship management. Day-to-day media relations, follow-up, and media training coordination keep the story moving rather than landing once and going quiet.
  5. Scope expansion as the career grows. Brand strategy, strategic partnerships, influencer engagement, and crisis communications get folded in as a client's public profile widens.

What to Look For in a Boutique Firm vs. a Volume Shop

A volume PR shop runs every client through the same press list and the same pitch template, because the business model depends on spreading limited hours across a large roster. A boutique firm built around emerging talent runs the opposite model: fewer clients, more hours per client, and a pitch built around where a specific career is, not a template. East 2 West Collective's Los Angeles and New York teams are built on the boutique model — personalized media lists, a single strong central pitch idea per client, and day-to-day relationship management rather than a one-and-done press release.
