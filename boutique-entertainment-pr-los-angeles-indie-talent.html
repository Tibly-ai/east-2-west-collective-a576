What a Los Angeles Engagement Actually Includes

A generic mass press list can't replicate the relationships LA entertainment press runs on — which editor covers which beat, which producer takes a cold pitch, which outlet actually moves the needle for a specific stage of career. East 2 West Collective's Los Angeles team builds campaigns from a working set of disciplines, pulled together into a blueprint for the client in front of them:

  • Broadcast, digital and print media relations — pitching and placing stories across TV, radio, online outlets and print.
  • Press release development and distribution — written, targeted, and sent to the right desks rather than a general wire blast.
  • Media training — preparing clients to walk into any interview ready and confident.
  • Entertainment publicity — for talent, TV networks and film, from established names to emerging ones.
  • Brand strategy and consultancy — messaging and positioning that keeps a brand top of mind between press cycles.
  • Crisis management — fast, clear-headed response when a client's story turns unexpected.

These aren't offered as isolated add-ons. On the Entertainment PR page, the firm describes building a brand persona that covers a client's career, philanthropic work, business endeavors, endorsement deals and partnerships — refined continuously as a campaign runs, not locked into a template at kickoff.

How the Process Works

A Los Angeles engagement starts with a scoping call to establish where a client's public profile actually sits today — not where a press kit says it should be. From there, the team builds a media list specific to the story and the outlets whose audiences actually sit with that kind of coverage, develops the pitch around one strong angle rather than a scattershot of talking points, and — for clients with a launch, release, or event on the calendar — identifies the right forum to give a story room to breathe beyond a single interview segment. That's the exact model the team used on the Justine Bateman book campaign (detailed below): a targeted media list, one central pitch angle, and a venue for the conversation to continue.

What to Look for in a Boutique LA Entertainment Publicist

Rising talent doesn't need a machine. The things worth checking before signing with any firm:

  • Does the team personalize the pitch, or run every client through the same list? A boutique shop built around emerging talent can spend more real hours on a single client's story instead of spreading attention across a roster of decades-established names.
  • Do they have a crisis protocol, not just a media relations plan? Crisis communications should be a named discipline on the list, not something improvised after the fact.
  • Can they show real, verifiable placements? Ask for a working log of actual segments and features, not a generic client-logo wall.
  • Do they operate where the client's story will actually run? East 2 West Collective runs campaigns out of both a Los Angeles office and a New York office — 745 Fifth Avenue, Suite 500, New York NY 10151 — so a client's coverage isn't limited to one coast's outlets.
