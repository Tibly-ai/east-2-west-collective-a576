How East 2 West Collective Handles Entertainment Publicity in Los Angeles

By East 2 West Collective · Updated October 9, 2026

East 2 West Collective runs entertainment publicity campaigns through a direct sequence: an initial conversation to understand the client, development of a tailored strategic blueprint, a media relations push across broadcast, digital and print, and ongoing refinement as placements come in. Every step is built around the individual client, not a packaged template. Call 310-963-8050 or email emuch@e2wcollective.com to start.

Entertainment publicity doesn't run on a script, and we don't pretend it does. What follows is the actual sequence we use with talent, television properties and film clients working with our entertainment publicity team, laid out step by step so you know what to expect before you ever pick up the phone.

1. First Contact and Initial Conversation

Every campaign starts with a conversation, not a questionnaire. You reach out — by phone at 310-963-8050 or by email at emuch@e2wcollective.com — and tell us who you are, where your career or project stands, and what you're trying to accomplish. We listen for the full picture: career trajectory, any philanthropic work, business endeavors, endorsement deals, and existing partnerships, since our approach to entertainment publicity maps a brand persona across all of it, not just the headline of the moment.

What you do: Come with a sense of where you are now and where you want media attention to take you. You don't need a polished pitch — that's our job.

What you get: A real conversation with the team about whether we're the right fit, and what a campaign built around you could look like.

2. Assessment of the Client and the Landscape

Before any strategy gets written, we assess the client against the current media environment. The TV and film landscape shifts constantly under new digital services, and a strategy that worked for someone else's campaign last year won't automatically work for yours. Our team includes strategists who specialize specifically in television networks and properties, individual shows, and feature films, so this assessment pulls in that specialized read on where a project or a career actually sits in the current landscape.

What you do: Share the material we need — bio, reel, project details, existing press, whatever positions us to evaluate the opportunity honestly.

What you get: A clear-eyed read on where you stand and what's realistic, before we build anything around you.

3. The Strategic Blueprint

This is where East 2 West Collective diverges from a one-size-fits-all shop. Every client gets their own strategic blueprint — not a plan copied from the last campaign. The blueprint maps the full picture: career, philanthropy, business endeavors, endorsement deals and partnerships, built to hold up across all of it rather than chase a single news cycle. For talent and properties, this draws on our full-spectrum capabilities, from media relations and press release development to crisis management and media training, assembled into whichever combination actually fits the client in front of us.

What you do: Review the blueprint, ask questions, and tell us what resonates or what's missing from your perspective.

What you get: A written strategic plan specific to you — not a generic entertainment PR checklist.

4. Scheduling and Setting the Campaign in Motion

Once the blueprint is approved, we move into scheduling: aligning the campaign against key dates — a premiere, a release, an award season, a press cycle you need to be ready for. For clients tied to a show or a feature, this step coordinates with network and production timelines so the publicity push lands when it matters, not after the window has closed.

What you do: Flag any fixed dates — air dates, release dates, event dates — that the campaign needs to build around.

What you get: A campaign calendar aligned to the moments that actually move the needle for your project.

5. The Work: Media Relations in Motion

This is the core of entertainment publicity: pitching and placing stories across TV, radio, online outlets and print. We go to the outlets whose audiences and formats fit the story, not a mass blast to every desk in the market. For established names and emerging talent alike, this is continuously refined — the blueprint isn't static, it's a living plan our team adjusts as placements land and the media conversation shifts. Our brand marketing and consultancy work runs on this same philosophy: nothing sits in a silo, and every piece of coverage is built to reinforce the next.

What you do: Stay available for interview opportunities as they're booked, and loop us in on anything newsworthy happening on your end.

What you get: Active media outreach on your behalf, with placements tracked as they come in.

6. Media Training, When It's Needed

Not every client needs media training, but when an interview opportunity calls for it, we prepare clients to walk in ready and confident. This is part of the broader toolkit we draw from — alongside crisis management for when a client's story turns unexpected, and strategic communications that keep messaging consistent across every appearance.

What you do: Take the prep seriously — it's built specifically around the interview or appearance in front of you.

What you get: A clear sense of your message and how to deliver it before cameras roll.

7. Ongoing Review and Refinement

Entertainment publicity isn't a single press release and a handshake. We continuously refine the blueprint as media exposure comes in, measuring what's landing and adjusting the pitch angles and target outlets accordingly. The goal stated plainly: media exposure that actually means something, not just column inches.

What you do: Stay in the loop on results and keep communicating shifts in your availability or priorities.

What you get: A campaign that evolves with you, rather than one locked to the plan we wrote in week one.

The same placements-first track record shows up across our media coverage log, spanning outlets from TODAY and Forbes to The New York Times and Variety.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens on the first call with East 2 West Collective?

We talk through where you or your project currently stand and what you want entertainment publicity to achieve. There's no required paperwork to start that conversation — just reach out by phone or email.

What happens if my campaign needs to align with a premiere or air date?

We build the campaign calendar around those fixed dates during the scheduling step, coordinating outreach so coverage lands ahead of and around the date that matters, rather than after it.

What happens if a client's story turns unexpected mid-campaign?

Crisis management is one of our core disciplines. We respond with a fast, clear-headed plan built for the specific situation rather than a generic holding statement.

What happens if I'm an emerging talent without major prior press?

The blueprint is built from where you are now, not where an established name would be. Our team has strategists focused on both established names and careers still being built.

What happens after the first round of media placements lands?

We don't close the file. The blueprint gets refined continuously based on what's working, and outreach continues toward the next round of coverage.

What happens if my project is a TV show or feature film rather than an individual?

That's handled by the strategists on our team who specialize in networks, individual shows and feature films, building an integrated cross-media campaign aimed at growing viewership and market position.
