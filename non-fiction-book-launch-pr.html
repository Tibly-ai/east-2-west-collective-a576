How Much Does It Cost to Hire a Boutique PR Firm for a Book Launch Campaign?

Typical monthly retainer: $4,000–$7,000. Typical minimum campaign length: 3 months.

That range and that minimum aren't arbitrary — a book launch is built around a fixed publication date (pre-launch positioning, a launch-week push, and post-launch follow-through), not a single month of scattershot pitching.

What Affects Your Price

  • How wide the media list needs to be. A national broadcast push costs more to build and run than a campaign focused on digital and regional print.
  • How much lead time exists before the pub date. A tighter runway compresses the same amount of work into less time — which changes the pace and intensity of the retainer, not just the calendar.
  • Whether imagery and press materials already exist, or need to be produced as part of onboarding.
  • The 3-month minimum commitment. A single month of pitching rarely produces the kind of placement a launch is built to land — a pub-date arc needs pre-launch positioning, a launch-week push, and post-launch follow-through to work.

How a Non-Fiction Book Launch Campaign Is Scoped

Every engagement starts the same way, whatever the runway looks like:

  • Media list build, scoped to category. Memoir, cultural criticism, and personal narrative each pull a different mix of national broadcast, digital, and print outlets — the list is built around the book's specific category before outreach begins.
  • One pitch, one central idea. Instead of a scattershot press release, the campaign is seeded around a single question or angle strong enough for an editor to say yes on a first read.
  • A pub-date arc. Positioning before launch, a coordinated push during launch week, and follow-through after — built backward from a release date that isn't moving.
  • Onboarding before pitching. Media list build and positioning work happen before a single pitch goes out, scoped to the specific book on the first call.

Authors working with us in New York get access to book launch campaigns run specifically out of the New York office, including national broadcast bookers and print desks headquartered there, alongside Los Angeles entertainment press reach. Authors based on the West Coast work the same model through book launch campaigns run out of the Los Angeles office.

What to Look For in a Boutique Literary PR Agency

A category-scoped media list and a single strong pitch angle are table stakes. Beyond that, the questions that actually separate firms:

  • Has the firm landed a non-fiction title on a national bestseller list, or is every claim about "media relationships" unverifiable?
  • Does the retainer include onboarding and positioning work before pitching starts, or is pitching sold as day one with no prep?
  • Is photo shoot coordination for author press imagery part of the conversation, or does it show up as a surprise line item mid-campaign?
  • Does the firm run both New York and Los Angeles desks directly, or does a national push require a handoff to a second agency?
