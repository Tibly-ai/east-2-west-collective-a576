What an Indie Film PR Campaign Actually Includes

Independent film doesn't get covered by accident. A festival premiere, a limited theatrical run, or a first-time director's debut needs the same discipline as a studio release — a media list built for the specific film, a pitch angle that gives outlets a reason to say yes, and a team that can pivot fast when a story doesn't land the way it was planned.

How an Engagement Works

Every indie film campaign at East 2 West Collective starts the same way, regardless of festival or budget:

  • A media list built around the film, not a template. The cast, the director's background, and the subject matter determine which outlets get pitched — a horror festival title and a social-issue documentary don't go to the same list.
  • One central pitch angle. Instead of a press release covering every detail of the film, the campaign is seeded around a single question or hook strong enough for an editor or festival reporter to say yes on a first read.
  • A timeline anchored to the festival or release date. Positioning work happens before the premiere, press attendance and outreach run during it, and follow-through coverage is pursued after — the same pub-date discipline the firm uses on its book launch campaigns.
  • Crisis readiness built in, not bolted on. Because crisis management is a standing specialization rather than an emergency add-on, a film facing an on-set story or a cast controversy during a release window has a team that's already thinking in those terms, not starting from zero.

What to Look For in an LA Indie Film Publicist

Producers and sales agents comparing firms for a festival run should be looking for three things, specifically:

  1. Relationships, not just a list. Which editor covers which beat, which producer takes a cold pitch, and which outlet actually moves the needle for an indie title at a specific festival — that's relationship work a generic mass press list can't replicate.
  2. A pitch built for the film. A generic release sent to a generic list is the fastest way for an indie title to get buried. The pitch angle has to come from the director's story, the cast, or the subject matter itself.
  3. A team that can pivot. Festival press is unpredictable — a story can fall through, a reviewer can pass, a controversy can surface overnight. The firm handling the campaign needs to be able to adjust mid-stream, not just execute a static plan.
